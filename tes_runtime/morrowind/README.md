# MorrowindRuntime

**Morrowind's conversations, running inside Skyrim.** Talk to the people of
Vvardenfell the way you did in 2002: topic lists, hyperlinked keywords,
disposition and all.

## What it does for you

Morrowind's dialogue is built very differently from Skyrim's. Characters
don't have a few fixed lines; they answer from thousands of shared topics
depending on who they are, where they stand, what faction you belong to and
how much they like you. Turning that into Skyrim dialogue would lose most of
it. MorrowindRuntime runs the original dialogue itself instead, built on the
open-source [OpenMW](https://openmw.org/) engine's own rules, so characters say
what they said in Morrowind for the same reasons.

- **Morrowind's dialogue window**, drawn from Morrowind's own art and font,
  with its topic list, clickable keywords and conversation history.
- **Disposition and persuasion.** Admire, intimidate, taunt and bribe people,
  with Morrowind's rules for how they react.
- **Services.** Silt striders, boats and guild guides take you places for a
  fare; merchants trade using Skyrim's own barter menu, and enchanters open
  Skyrim's enchanting menu (this one needs TESRuntime too).
- **Quests and the journal.** Dialogue advances quests and writes the journal
  as it did in Morrowind.
- **A living world.** Morrowind's scripts run on doors, traps, creatures and
  quest items: things react when you pick them up, open them, walk near them
  or kill them.
- **Crime.** Morrowind's guards, bounties and fines work through Skyrim's own
  crime system, so paying up, going to jail and resisting arrest all behave
  as you'd expect.

Skyrim's own characters and every other converted game keep Skyrim's normal
dialogue; only Morrowind characters use this window.

## Should I keep it enabled?

Yes, if you play Morrowind or Tamriel Rebuilt. Without it, Morrowind's
characters have nothing to say and most quests can't be started. It does
nothing for other games.

Your progress (journal, disposition, script state) is kept with your save, so
keep it enabled for as long as you play those saves.

## For developers

It runs Morrowind's dialogue natively by porting OpenMW's dialogue engine and
MWScript VM into an SKSE plugin, rather than reshaping TES3 dialogue into TES5
records.

- Everything: [morrowind_runtime.md](../../docs/commentary/morrowind_runtime.md)
- Licensing: it links vendored **GPL-3.0** OpenMW (`external/openmw/`), which is
  why it is its own DLL. It also compiles the MIT `../common/` sources, which
  MIT allows. [morrowind_runtime.md](../../docs/commentary/morrowind_runtime.md#licensing)

Reads `Data\SKSE\Plugins\MorrowindRuntime\<plugin>\` (staged by
`tes5_import/dialogue/morrowind_sidecar.py`). The enchanting service opens its
bench through TESRuntime's export (`../common/crafting_client.cpp`,
[tes_runtime_alchemy.md](../../docs/commentary/tes_runtime_alchemy.md#crafting-bench)).
Co-save owner `'MWRT'`. Log:
`MorrowindRuntime.log`; set `TESRUNTIME_VERBOSE` for per-tick script lines. The
menu movie `interface/morrowind_dialogue.swf` is generated by
`tools/generators/gen_morrowind_menu_swf.py`.

Source is in `plugin/`, namespace `tesruntime::mw`; headless test data is in
`testdata/`.

- `build.bat` → `..\dist\MorrowindRuntime.dll` (C++20, compiles the vendored
  OpenMW subset first).
- `build.bat openmw` compiles only the vendored OpenMW subset.
- `build.bat test` builds the headless tests: `store_test`, `filter_test`,
  `script_test` (each prints `OK` / `all passed`) and
  `session_test` (a manual tool: `<sidecar root> <actor id>`). Run them from
  this folder: they read `testdata\` by relative path.
